04 / 05 · Shared-stock projection
Interpretation

Disclosures reveal overlap
in reported purchasing

The network makes common stock choices visible and identifies the tickers that connect the widest group of filers.

Overlap alone cannot establish coordination, privileged information, or investment performance.

This extract excludes sales, other asset types, and unresolved tickers. Disclosures can include spouse or dependent transactions. Dollar amounts are reported in ranges. Missing records and ticker resolution can change the network.

3:00–3:40. The useful finding is descriptive: we can see which tickers connect the widest group of filers and how those purchases form an overlapping network. That creates a starting point for further reporting. A stronger investigation would compare purchase timing, household ownership, market popularity, and committee context before making claims about why any overlap occurs.
05 / 05 · Takeaway and limitations

Sources and calculation notes

GitHub repository · Full methods and findings · Data provenance

Adapted from Sean Ryan’s existing congressional stock-network project. Frozen source: 2025 accepted purchase extract. Original records: House financial disclosures. Prepared November 8, 2026. This presentation reanalyzes the existing snapshot rather than refreshing the underlying scrape.

Filters: transaction_type=P, asset_type=ST, ticker_parse_status=accepted, transaction year=2025, nonempty ticker. Strip Hon./Honorable from names and uppercase tickers, matching the original project. Unique politician–ticker pairs define unweighted two-mode ties. Retain tickers with at least two buyers. The undirected politician projection joins filers sharing a retained ticker. Density = observed projected ties / all possible filer pairs. Committee links are outside the metrics shown here.
